Where we work around Dumont, NJ

Our base is 40 Washington Avenue in Dumont, a borough of about two square miles in Bergen County with the single ZIP code 07628. From there, crews serve Dumont first and then five neighboring towns that border it or sit within a short drive.

Each town has its own page because the water problems differ. New Milford and Oradell sit on the Hackensack River, Cresskill and Demarest follow the Tenakill Brook, and Haworth borders the reservoir. A burst pipe is a burst pipe anywhere, but what comes with a storm or a rising river depends on where you are.

Dumont borders six municipalities, and five of them have pages here. The sixth, Bergenfield, is a nearby town we serve but do not list separately.

Haworth, NJ

North of Dumont, about 1 mile. ZIP 07641.

Haworth sits on the Oradell Reservoir side of Bergen County and has the smallest housing stock of our five nearby towns.

Cresskill, NJ

East of Dumont, about 1 mile. ZIP 07626.

Cresskill is split from Dumont by Knickerbocker Road, County Route 505, and the Tenakill Brook runs through it.

New Milford, NJ

Southwest of Dumont, about 2 miles. ZIP 07646.

New Milford lies on the Hackensack River, which has a flood gauge in the borough.

Demarest, NJ

Northeast of Dumont, about 2 miles. ZIP 07627.

Demarest is built around the Duck Pond on the Tenakill Brook and shares a high school with Haworth.

Oradell, NJ

West of Dumont, about 2 miles. ZIP 07649.

Oradell has a reservoir and a dam on the Hackensack River, and Kinderkamack Road runs through its middle.

Areas inside Dumont

Dumont has no officially named neighborhoods, so we describe areas by landmarks. Downtown Dumont covers the town center around Washington Avenue and Madison Avenue, where buildings often share walls. East Dumont covers the east side of the borough around Memorial Park.

Both pages share ZIP code 07628, and neither claims official boundaries. If your street sits between them, call the line and give your address.

How a call from a nearby town works

Call the 24/7 line and give your address and ZIP code. The person on the line asks what is wet, where the water started and whether it is still running, then arranges a crew. A same-day response is offered when a crew is available, and dispatch runs around the clock.

Please keep your own safety first. Close the main shutoff if you can, stay away from water that touches electrical outlets, and call the electric utility or 911 for any electrical or gas risk before you call us.

We do not promise arrival times, because traffic, weather and call volume change them. We do promise to explain the scope and the price before work starts.

Tell the dispatcher the nearest cross street and whether a basement, crawl space or upper floor is wet. That short description lets the crew bring the right pumps and drying equipment on the first trip.

What to have ready when you call

A few details help a crew plan the right equipment before they arrive. You do not need all of them, and you should never delay calling to gather them.

  • Your street address and ZIP code.
  • Where the water started, if you know, and whether it is still running.
  • Which floors and rooms are wet, and roughly how deep the water is.
  • Whether water has reached outlets, the electrical panel or a gas appliance.
  • Whether the water looks clean, gray or dark, and whether it smells.
