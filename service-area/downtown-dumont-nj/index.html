Where Downtown Dumont is, and how we use the name

Dumont is a Bergen County borough of 1.95 square miles, and it has no officially named neighborhoods. This page uses Downtown Dumont for the town center around the corner of Washington Avenue and Madison Avenue, near Borough Hall and Fred Triplett Park. The edges are approximate, and the whole borough shares ZIP code 07628.

The Old North Reformed Church, built in 1801, stands at that corner, according to the borough's own history page. Fred Triplett Park, dedicated to a soldier killed in 1944, sits near the town center. We mention them so you can place the area, not because they tell us anything about your pipes.

If water is spreading in a Downtown Dumont home, apartment or shop right now, call our 24/7 line at (844) 950-6900.

What is different about a town center water loss

In a town center, buildings often share walls, ceilings and floors. A leak in one unit can reach the unit below or beside it, and the first job is working out who is affected. We cannot tell you which buildings in this area are mixed-use, so describe yours when you call.

That description matters for the work. A supply line failing in an upstairs bathroom is a different problem from a sprinkler or roof leak above a shop. Tell the dispatcher how many floors and units are involved, who owns the building and whether anyone is living there.

First response in a shared building

These steps keep people safe while you wait for a crew:

  1. Keep people and pets away from standing water that may touch outlets, appliances or wiring.
  2. Shut off the water supply only if the valve is dry and easy to reach.
  3. Do not turn breakers on or off while standing in water. Call the electric utility or 911 for electrical danger.
  4. Photograph the damage, the source and any affected belongings when it is safe.
  5. Move unaffected belongings out of the wet area if you can do so safely.
  6. Tell your landlord, property manager or neighbors below you so they can check their own space.

Water from a sewage backup or outside flooding can carry contamination. Do not use fans, a household vacuum or do-it-yourself drying on it.

How extraction and drying work in a building like this

Extraction comes first. Crews pump or vacuum standing water and lift what cannot be dried, such as soaked carpet pad. Then they set air movers and dehumidifiers, and they take moisture readings at fixed points so progress is measured and not guessed.

In a shared building, drying often has to continue in the unit that leaked and in the one below. Ceilings and wall cavities may need to be opened so air reaches wet insulation and framing. Our structural drying and dehumidification page explains how readings tell a crew when to stop.

The published standard behind this work is ANSI/IICRC S500. You can ask any company whether its crews follow it, and you should ask for the readings when the job ends.

Insurance and documentation

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and the terms of your policy. Check your policy and ask your insurer what it covers. Renters, tenants, owners and landlords usually carry different kinds of coverage, so ask which one applies to you.

Photos, a list of what was damaged and daily moisture readings can help describe the loss. They do not guarantee that a claim will be approved. We do not decide coverage, and we cannot promise what an insurer will pay.
