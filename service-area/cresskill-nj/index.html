Historic houses and what Cresskill's housing means for a leak

Cresskill became a borough in 1894, and several of its houses are on the National Register of Historic Places, including the Blackledge-Gair House on Madison Avenue and the Demarest-Atwood House on Jefferson Avenue. We do not know the plumbing or finishes of any particular house, so we ask about yours when you call.

The practical point is to know your own house. Find the main shutoff and test it, and if you are unsure about your supply lines, ask a plumber to look at them before something fails. Plaster and lath walls, where a house has them, dry slowly.

The borough had 3,157 housing units in the 2020 Census, a small stock, so each house tends to have its own history. We ask about it when you call.

Water is still spreading? Do this before we arrive

Pick the steps that apply and stay safe.

Shut the valve

Find the shutoff, usually near where the line enters the house, and turn it clockwise.

Check the panel

If water has reached the panel or outlets, stay away and call the utility or 911.

Save what you can

Lift contents off wet floors and photograph the water first.

Contain the area

Close doors to the wet room and keep people and pets out of dirty water.

Brooks, streets and the places that shape a call
