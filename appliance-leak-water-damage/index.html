An appliance is leaking: unplug it and close the valve

An appliance leak can start slowly and flood a floor by morning. Treat it as an emergency. Our line is open 24/7. Unplug the appliance only if you can reach the cord without stepping in water. Otherwise turn off the breaker for that circuit.

Close the water valve behind or beneath it. For a washing machine, that is the two hot and cold valves on the wall. For a dishwasher, it is usually under the sink. For a refrigerator with an ice maker, it is the small line on the back or under the sink.

Pull the appliance out only if it is safe, then lift mats and rugs, and call. Water under an appliance runs under cabinets and into the subfloor, where you cannot see it.

How appliance leaks hide until they are big

A hose that weeps can spread water under vinyl or laminate without any pooling. The first sign is a floor that feels soft, edges that lift, or a musty smell. By then the subfloor may be wet.

A washing machine fill hose, left under pressure for years, can split suddenly and spray. A dishwasher drain hose or door seal can drip slowly. A refrigerator ice maker line can leak behind the unit for weeks. The slow ones cause the most hidden damage.

What the crew checks after an appliance leak

The visible puddle is only part of the job. A crew checks:

  • Flooring above the subfloor, including the edges where water travels.
  • The back and base of the cabinets near the appliance.
  • Drywall behind and beside the unit.
  • The ceiling below, if the appliance is on an upper floor.
  • The supply line and valve, so you know where the leak started.

Drying the floor beneath the appliance

The crew extracts water, lifts or removes flooring when needed, and sets air movers and drying mats under the cabinet edges. Dehumidifiers lower the moisture in the air so materials can release water.

Vinyl and laminate often need to come up because water trapped beneath them does not escape. Tile may survive, but the grout and the subfloor below are checked. Hardwood follows the process in our hardwood floor water damage repair page.

Cost and insurance for appliance leaks

Price depends on how long the leak ran, how much flooring is involved and whether cabinets or walls were reached. A fast catch with dry subfloor is a small job. A long hidden leak under a kitchen floor is much bigger.

Sudden failures of an appliance supply line are often covered by homeowners insurance, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. The appliance itself usually is not covered. The standard that guides drying after an appliance leak

When a washer hose or dishwasher line fails, crews dry the structure the way ANSI/IICRC S500 describes. That means finding the wet material, controlling humidity and checking readings until they match a dry reference.

You can ask a company which published standard its crews follow. A claim to follow a standard is not a certification claim, so ask how it shows up in the daily records.

What to do before help arrives

A leaking appliance can be made safe in a few minutes:

  • Unplug the appliance only if the outlet and the floor are dry.
  • Close the shutoff valve behind or beside the appliance, or the main valve.
  • Do not pull a heavy appliance across wet flooring, which can tear it.
  • Mop up standing water and photograph the damage.
  • Do not run the appliance again until a technician checks it.
