Water Damage Insurance Claims: Documentation and Steps in Dumont, NJ

A claim goes better when the first hours are documented. Here are the steps in order, from stopping the water to the adjuster's visit.

By the Dumont Water Damage Pros team · November 12, 2026

Step one is stopping more damage, not paperwork

Insurance claims start the moment water gets in. The first thing to do is stop it, because most policies expect you to protect the property from further harm. Close the main shutoff, turn off power where water is near outlets, and move valuables out of the wet area.

Then, before anything is thrown out or moved around too much, take photos. A claim is easier to describe when the adjuster can see what you saw. If someone is in danger, forget the photos and get safe first.

The claim in order

Every claim differs, but this sequence works for most sudden losses:

  1. Stop the source and make the area safe.
  2. Photograph and video the water, the source and the damage, wide and close.
  3. Call your insurer or agent to report the loss, and note the date, time and the name of the person you spoke to.
  4. Ask whether you may begin emergency drying and what records they want.
  5. Call a restoration company so drying starts quickly. It limits damage and often limits the claim.
  6. Keep damaged items you can safely store, or photograph them before disposal.
  7. Keep receipts for emergency repairs, hotel stays and supplies.
  8. Meet the adjuster, share your photos, and ask for the scope in writing.

Working with the adjuster

An adjuster is the insurer's representative who looks at the loss and estimates what is covered. Be polite and clear, show your photos and logs, and ask questions. If you disagree with the scope, ask for the reasoning in writing.

Get a copy of the adjuster's estimate. Compare it with the restoration company's scope. Differences are common, and they can often be resolved by sharing the moisture readings and photos that justify the work.

If new damage appears later, such as a stain that shows up after the drying, tell the insurer promptly. Many policies allow supplemental claims, but check your own wording for time limits.

Mistakes that make claims harder

A few habits create trouble. Throwing out damaged items before photographing them removes evidence. Waiting several days to report the loss can raise questions about whether the damage grew. Starting major demolition before the insurer has a chance to see anything may complicate the scope.

At the same time, do not wait for the adjuster before you dry the structure. Water left sitting makes the claim larger, and EPA guidance says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. Ask the insurer what to do, and document as you go.

Keeping a simple claim file

A claim goes more smoothly when everything is in one place. Use a folder on paper or on your phone and add to it as you go. Include photos and video, the date and time you found the damage, the claim number, the adjuster's name and phone number, receipts and the contractor's scope.

Write down every call with your insurer, including the date, who you spoke with and what was said. Follow important calls with an email so there is a written record. If anything is unclear, ask your insurer to explain it in writing.

Questions worth asking your insurer

Policies differ, so ask directly:

  • Does my policy cover this cause of loss?
  • What is my deductible for this claim?
  • What documents do you need, and by when?
  • Will you inspect before repairs begin?
  • How does the policy treat mold that follows water damage?

Industry references that support your records

Drying records that follow ANSI/IICRC S500 practice, such as daily readings and equipment logs, give an insurer clear evidence of what was done. Ask your contractor to share them. Your insurer decides coverage, and the records help the conversation.
